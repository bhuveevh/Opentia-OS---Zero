The Story of Zero

Why Opentia’s first release is called “Zero”.

Over 1,500 years ago, ancient Indian mathematicians like Aryabhata revolutionised arithmetic by treating zero — shunya — as both a symbol and a numeral in its own right. It was an idea so powerful that it re-wrote how humanity counts, calculates, and thinks.

The modern decimal system, and the foundations of algebra, trigonometry, and calculus, all trace heavily back to ancient India. Every computer on Earth — every line of code, every “1” and “0” — runs on that inheritance.

“Zero is not nothing. Zero is the beginning of everything you build on it.”

Opentia OS starts the same way: from zero — no name, no branding, just a working system — and grows into something of its own. That is why the very first release carries the codename Zero. The nine dots in the Opentia logo circle around that idea: a zero, made of many small parts, moving together.
